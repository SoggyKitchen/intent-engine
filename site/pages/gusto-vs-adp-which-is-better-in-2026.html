Gusto vs ADP: The Core Difference

Gusto was built for the small business owner — you can run payroll in minutes, benefits are integrated, and the interface doesn't require training. ADP was built for payroll processing at scale — it handles the most complex compliance scenarios but at the cost of simplicity.

The typical switch: a growing startup using ADP because "it's what accountants use" discovers Gusto and moves — because the experience is dramatically better and the pricing is comparable or lower for small headcounts.

Our Final Verdict (July 2026)

Gusto wins for small and growing businesses. Here is the breakdown:

  • Choose Gusto if: you have 1–100 employees, want a clean and fast payroll experience, need integrated benefits without a PEO, or are currently running payroll on spreadsheets or QuickBooks manually.
  • Choose ADP if: you have 100+ employees with complex multi-state compliance, union contracts, prevailing wage requirements, or need the deepest available ACA reporting tools. Also relevant if your accountant or ERP has existing ADP integrations.
  • Freelancers/contractors only: Gusto wins with its $6/contractor/mo plan (no base fee).
  • Switching from ADP: Many small businesses find the migration to Gusto saves time and money — Gusto's migration team handles the transition from ADP data.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Gusto better than ADP for small business?
Yes — for most small businesses (under 100 employees), Gusto scores 8.6/10 vs ADP's 7.8/10. Gusto has a cleaner interface, transparent pricing, better employee self-service, and faster setup. ADP is better for businesses with complex compliance needs or union payroll.
How much does Gusto cost vs ADP?
Gusto Simple: $40/mo + $6/employee. Gusto Plus: $80/mo + $12/employee. ADP Run starts at ~$59/mo + $4/employee. For 10 employees: Gusto = $100/mo vs ADP ≈ $99/mo — similar cost, but Gusto includes more (HR tools, self-onboarding, benefits integration). ADP pricing is often negotiable.
Does Gusto do automatic tax filing like ADP?
Yes — Gusto automatically files federal, state, and local payroll taxes on all plans. ADP also files taxes automatically. Both provide year-end W-2 and 1099 filing. Gusto is considered easier for this workflow; ADP has broader tools for complex multi-state scenarios.
Which is easier to use — Gusto or ADP?
Gusto is significantly easier. Business owners regularly complete payroll in minutes. ADP Run's interface is older and more complex, often requiring support from ADP's team during setup. Gusto's setup can be completed in a few hours without outside help.
Can I try Gusto or ADP for free?
Gusto doesn't offer a free trial but has a 30-day money-back guarantee. ADP Run offers a 3-month free trial for new small business customers. We recommend trying both before committing — the user experience difference becomes obvious quickly.
Does ADP have better benefits than Gusto?
At enterprise scale, ADP has broader benefits through TotalSource PEO. For small businesses, Gusto's built-in benefits (health insurance in all 50 states, 401k via Guideline, HSA/FSA, commuter benefits) are more integrated and easier to manage than ADP's add-on benefits.
