Who Are These Tools For?

Both FreshBooks and Wave target small businesses and freelancers in the Accounting Software category, but they serve different budget and complexity needs. The decision often comes down to one question: can you justify a monthly subscription?

FreshBooks is the right fit if you're billing clients regularly, need time tracking baked in, want polished client-facing proposals, or require expense receipts captured on mobile. At $17/mo (Lite, up to 5 clients) it's an investment — but for a service business billing even $500/mo, it pays for itself quickly in time saved and more professional invoices.

Wave is the right fit if you're just starting out, have very simple books, or genuinely cannot budget a subscription. Its core features — invoicing, double-entry accounting, income/expense tracking, and financial reports — are free forever. Wave Payroll ($20/mo + $6/employee) and Wave Payments (pay-per-use fees) are the only costs.

Where FreshBooks Pulls Ahead

  • Time tracking: Built into every plan. Track time by client or project, then convert directly to an invoice in one click. Wave has no native time tracking.
  • Proposals and contracts: FreshBooks lets you send proposals and get e-signatures — essential for service businesses. Wave does not offer this.
  • Project management: FreshBooks includes basic project tracking with budgets and team collaboration. Wave is invoice-only beyond core bookkeeping.
  • Receipt capture: Mobile app photo-to-expense on all plans. Wave supports receipt scanning via Wave Receipts (separate app).
  • Client portal: Clients can pay invoices, review proposals, and leave comments through a branded FreshBooks portal. Wave's client experience is more basic.
  • Customer support: Phone and chat support on all FreshBooks plans. Wave offers email-only support on the free tier; phone support is a paid add-on.

Where Wave Holds Its Own

  • Price (it's free): For businesses that genuinely only need invoicing and basic bookkeeping, $0 beats $17/mo every time.
  • Unlimited invoices and clients on free plan: FreshBooks Lite caps at 5 billable clients. Wave has no such limit — useful for freelancers with many small, occasional clients.
  • Wave Money (US only): An integrated business bank account that connects directly to your books — a genuinely useful feature for US sole traders.
  • Double-entry accounting: Wave uses proper double-entry bookkeeping, so your Chart of Accounts and reports are accountant-ready. It's not a toy.

Integrations Worth Knowing About

FreshBooks connects to 100+ apps including Stripe, Shopify, HubSpot, G Suite, Slack, Trello, Basecamp, and Asana. This makes it genuinely useful as the financial hub of a small business tech stack.

Wave's integration library is much smaller — Etsy, PayPal, Shoeboxed, and Zapier cover the basics. If you're already using Wave and need a missing integration, Zapier can bridge many gaps, but it adds complexity and cost.

Mobile Apps

FreshBooks' mobile app (iOS and Android) is genuinely capable: snap receipts, log time, create and send invoices, accept payments, and reply to clients — all from your phone. It's one of the better accounting mobile apps on the market.

Wave's mobile app handles invoicing and receipt capture adequately, but lacks time tracking and project management because those features don't exist in Wave at all. For many freelancers, that's fine.

Our Final Verdict: FreshBooks vs Wave

This comparison has a clear winner for most users, but the right answer depends entirely on your situation.

Choose FreshBooks if:

  • You invoice clients regularly and want time tracked automatically against those invoices
  • You send proposals, quotes, or contracts and want e-signatures built in
  • You have a team and need project budgets, task tracking, and collaboration
  • You want automated late payment reminders and a branded client portal
  • You need phone or chat support when something goes wrong
  • You're billing enough to justify $17–$55/mo (most service businesses are)

Choose Wave if:

  • Your budget is genuinely $0 and basic invoicing + bookkeeping is all you need
  • You have more than 5 clients but only need simple invoices (FreshBooks Lite caps at 5)
  • You want native payroll without a third-party integration (US/Canada)
  • You're in the US and want a built-in business bank account (Wave Money)
  • You're a sole trader with very simple accounts and no employees

Frequently Asked Questions

Is FreshBooks better than Wave?
FreshBooks (8.9/10) is better for service-based businesses that invoice clients, track time, and need proposal and contract features. Wave (7.8/10) is better if budget is zero — its free accounting and invoicing covers basic needs. FreshBooks wins on features; Wave wins on price.
Does Wave really cost nothing?
Wave's core accounting, invoicing, and financial reporting are genuinely free. You pay only for optional add-ons: Wave Payroll ($20/mo + $6/employee), Wave Payments (transaction fees only), and premium support. There's no subscription for basic bookkeeping.
Which is better for freelancers?
Depends on client volume. Wave handles unlimited invoices free — ideal for freelancers just starting out. FreshBooks Lite ($17/mo, 5 clients) adds time tracking, proposal tools, and receipt capture, making it worth it once you have regular clients and want a more professional workflow.
Can FreshBooks and Wave integrate with my bank?
Both offer bank connections. FreshBooks connects to 14,000+ banks for transaction imports and auto-reconciliation. Wave offers Wave Money (US only) — a business banking account built in. Both support manual CSV import as a fallback.
Does FreshBooks have a free plan?
FreshBooks does not have a permanent free plan, but it offers a 30-day free trial with full access to all features — no credit card required. After the trial, paid plans start at $17/mo (Lite, up to 5 billable clients).
Which is better for a small business with employees?
Wave has native payroll built in ($20/mo + $6/employee in the US and Canada), which is a genuine advantage if you need payroll without a third-party integration. FreshBooks integrates with Gusto for payroll at extra cost. For everything beyond payroll — project tracking, time logging, client billing — FreshBooks is the stronger platform.
