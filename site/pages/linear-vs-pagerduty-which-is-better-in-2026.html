Frequently Asked Questions

Is Linear better than PagerDuty?

It depends on your needs. Linear excels in agile issue tracking and developer experience, while PagerDuty is unmatched in incident response and on-call management. They serve different primary functions.

Which is cheaper, Linear or PagerDuty?

Linear’s Pro plan starts at $10/user/month, while PagerDuty’s Standard plan starts at $9/user/month. However, PagerDuty’s full feature set requires the $29/user/month Professional tier, making Linear more cost-effective for teams needing only issue tracking.

Can I switch from PagerDuty to Linear?

Yes, but only if you’re replacing issue tracking, not incident response. You can migrate issues via CSV or API, but Linear doesn’t support on-call scheduling, so full replacement isn’t recommended for ops-heavy teams.

Does Linear or PagerDuty have a free plan?

Yes, both offer free plans. Linear’s free tier supports up to 3 users with core features. PagerDuty’s free plan includes basic alerting and one on-call schedule but lacks advanced automation and reporting.

Which has better customer support, Linear or PagerDuty?

PagerDuty offers 24/7 phone and chat support on higher tiers with strict SLAs, ideal for enterprise needs. Linear provides email and in-app support with faster response times for Enterprise customers, but no phone support.

Is Linear or PagerDuty better for small teams?

Linear is better for small engineering teams focused on building software quickly. PagerDuty is overkill unless the team runs critical infrastructure requiring 24/7 monitoring and incident response.

Does Linear integrate with PagerDuty?

Yes, Linear integrates with PagerDuty via webhook or automation tools like Zapier. You can trigger PagerDuty alerts from Linear issues, enabling hybrid workflows between development and operations.

Which tool has more features, Linear or PagerDuty?

PagerDuty has more features overall, especially in monitoring, alerting, and incident lifecycle management. Linear focuses on fewer, deeply optimized features for issue tracking and agile development, offering superior UX in that domain.

Feature Deep Dive

Linear shines with features like 'Cycles' for sprint planning, 'Inbox' for issue triage, and 'States' for customizable workflows. Its 'Quick Find' and keyboard navigation drastically reduce friction for developers. PagerDuty, in contrast, offers 'Incident Workflows', 'On-Call Scheduling', 'Response Plays', and 'War Rooms' for real-time incident collaboration. While Linear streamlines development, PagerDuty automates and escalates critical system alerts with precision, making it indispensable for SREs managing high-availability systems.

Pricing Breakdown

Linear’s Pro plan costs $10/user/month (billed annually) and includes unlimited issues, cycles, and integrations. The Enterprise plan adds SSO, audit logs, and enhanced support. PagerDuty’s Standard plan is $9/user/month but lacks event orchestration; the Professional plan at $29/user/month includes automation, incident analytics, and response coordination. For a 10-person team, Linear costs $1,200/year vs PagerDuty’s $3,480/year for full functionality, making Linear significantly more affordable for non-critical use cases.

Who Should Use Linear

Linear is ideal for product-driven engineering teams of 5–100 people who prioritize speed, clean design, and agile workflows. It suits startups and mid-market tech companies building software rapidly without the overhead of enterprise incident management. Teams with limited ops needs but high development velocity will benefit most from Linear’s intuitive interface and automation.

Who Should Use PagerDuty

PagerDuty is best for large enterprises, SRE teams, and IT operations managing 24/7 systems requiring high reliability. It’s essential for organizations in finance, healthcare, or cloud services where downtime is costly. Teams needing on-call rotations, automated escalations, and deep monitoring integrations will find PagerDuty’s robust platform indispensable despite its complexity.

Migration & Setup

Switching from PagerDuty to Linear is feasible only if deprecating incident management. Linear offers CSV and API imports for issues, with typical onboarding in under a week. Migrating from Linear to PagerDuty requires setting up monitoring integrations and on-call policies, taking 2–4 weeks for full deployment. Both tools provide documentation and support, but PagerDuty’s setup is inherently more complex due to its operational scope.
