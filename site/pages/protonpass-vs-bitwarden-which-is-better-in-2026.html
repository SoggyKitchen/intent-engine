Verdict at a Glance

Both Proton Pass and Bitwarden solve the same core problem: storing passwords securely with strong encryption. The decision comes down to two factors.

Price: Bitwarden's free plan does more for free (unlimited devices, better sharing), and its premium tier at $10/year is one of the lowest in the category. Proton Pass free includes 10 email aliases but lacks TOTP code storage — that requires Proton Pass Plus at $35.88/year.

Ecosystem: If you use ProtonMail or ProtonVPN, Proton Unlimited ($11.99/mo) includes all Proton products including Proton Pass Plus, making it excellent value. For standalone password manager use, Bitwarden wins on price and raw feature depth.

The email alias feature is Proton Pass's genuine differentiator — no comparable password manager includes this natively. If protecting your email address from data breaches is a priority, Proton Pass earns its premium.

Who Should Choose Proton Pass?

  • Proton ecosystem users — If you pay for Proton Unlimited ($11.99/mo), Proton Pass Plus is included at no extra cost.
  • Email privacy priority — Hide-my-email aliases let you sign up to sites with a unique masked address, so breaches at third parties never expose your real email.
  • Swiss jurisdiction preference — Data stored under Swiss privacy law, with Proton's track record of resisting government requests.
  • All-in-one privacy stack — ProtonMail + ProtonVPN + ProtonDrive + Proton Pass from one provider.

Who Should Choose Bitwarden?

  • Budget-first users — Free plan covers unlimited passwords on unlimited devices; premium at $10/year is hard to beat.
  • Self-hosters — Bitwarden can be self-hosted on your own server for maximum control. Proton Pass cannot.
  • Teams and organizations — Bitwarden Teams at $3/user/mo and Enterprise at $5/user/mo offer robust admin controls, SSO, and compliance features.
  • Vault health reports (premium) — Identifies weak, reused, and compromised passwords across your vault. Proton Pass does not have an equivalent at the plus tier.
  • Browser extension maturity — Bitwarden's browser extensions are more mature and have broader browser support including niche browsers.

The Email Alias Advantage

The single biggest differentiator for Proton Pass is hide-my-email aliases. When you create a new account anywhere, Proton Pass can generate a unique email alias (e.g. randomstring@passmail.com) that forwards to your real inbox. If that service is breached, you delete the alias and your real email is never exposed.

Bitwarden simply does not have this feature. To get equivalent functionality alongside Bitwarden, you would need a separate SimpleLogin, AnonAddy, or Apple Hide My Email subscription — adding cost and complexity.

Our Final Verdict

For most users: Bitwarden

Bitwarden's $10/year premium is the best-value paid password manager. The free plan — unlimited passwords, unlimited devices — beats Proton Pass free on breadth. If you need TOTP storage, vault health reports, or self-hosting, Bitwarden wins without question.

For Proton ecosystem users: Proton Pass

If you pay $11.99/mo for Proton Unlimited, you already own Proton Pass Plus. Use it — the email alias feature alone is worth it, and avoiding a separate password manager subscription simplifies your stack. For pure privacy tooling (Swiss jurisdiction, end-to-end encryption across all your data), the Proton bundle is hard to match.

When to Choose Proton Pass

  • You use ProtonMail, ProtonVPN, or ProtonDrive (bundle value)
  • You want email aliases built into your password autofill flow
  • Swiss jurisdiction is important for your threat model
  • You want a single privacy-focused vendor for all credential-adjacent data

When to Choose Bitwarden

  • You want the most affordable premium password manager ($10/yr)
  • You need self-hosting for compliance or data residency requirements
  • You need team or enterprise SSO (SAML) features
  • You want vault health reports and emergency access at the premium tier
  • You prefer a US-based company with SOC 2 Type II compliance

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Proton Pass better than Bitwarden?
It depends on your priority. Bitwarden scores 8.8/10 vs Proton Pass's 8.5/10 in our scoring — Bitwarden wins on price ($10/year vs $35.88/year for premium), open-source trust with self-hosting, and vault health reports. Proton Pass wins if you want built-in email aliases or are already in the Proton ecosystem.
How much does Proton Pass cost vs Bitwarden?
Bitwarden Personal Premium is $10/year ($0.83/mo). Proton Pass Plus is $35.88/year ($2.99/mo, billed annually). Both have free tiers — Bitwarden free allows unlimited passwords on unlimited devices; Proton Pass free allows unlimited passwords and 10 hide-my-email aliases. If you pay for Proton Unlimited ($11.99/mo), Proton Pass Plus is included.
Does Proton Pass have email aliases like SimpleLogin?
Yes. Proton acquired SimpleLogin in 2022 and built hide-my-email aliases directly into Proton Pass. The free plan includes 10 aliases; the paid plan includes unlimited aliases. Bitwarden does not have this feature.
Can I try Proton Pass for free?
Yes. Proton Pass has a permanent free tier — unlimited passwords, 10 hide-my-email email aliases, and basic 2FA support on all devices. The Proton Pass Plus upgrade ($2.99/mo annual) adds unlimited email aliases, integrated TOTP 2FA codes, dark web monitoring, and priority support.
Is Bitwarden really free?
Yes. Bitwarden's free plan is genuinely generous: unlimited passwords, unlimited devices, unlimited item types (logins, cards, identities, secure notes), basic 2FA, and unlimited sharing with one other person. The premium upgrade at $10/year adds advanced 2FA (TOTP, hardware keys), encrypted file storage, vault health reports, and emergency access.
Can Bitwarden be self-hosted?
Yes. Bitwarden offers an official self-hosted version — you run the server on your own infrastructure and your vault data never leaves your environment. Proton Pass does not support self-hosting; your data is stored on Proton's Swiss servers.
