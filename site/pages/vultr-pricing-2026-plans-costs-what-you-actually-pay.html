Vultr Review

Vultr is a cloud infrastructure platform offering high-performance virtual machines, bare metal servers, and managed databases for developers and B2B tech teams. It's ideal for startups, SaaS companies, and enterprises needing scalable, global cloud hosting with predictable pricing.

Pros

  • Global network with 32+ regions for low-latency deployments
  • Hourly billing with no long-term commitment
  • Transparent, pay-as-you-go pricing with no hidden fees

Cons

  • Limited enterprise support on lower tiers
  • Fewer managed services compared to AWS or Google Cloud

Pricing: Starter: $2.50/month (1 CPU, 512MB RAM, 10GB SSD) High Frequency: $60/month (2 vCPU, 4GB RAM, 80GB NVMe) Dedicated Cloud: $160/month (4 vCPU, 8GB RAM, 160GB SSD) Bare Metal: $144/month (Intel Xeon, 64GB RAM, 2x1TB NVMe) Object Storage: $6/month (250GB) Managed Databases: from $15/month All plans billed hourly with no setup fees.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does Vultr cost per month?

Vultr starts at $2.50/month for the Nano instance (1 CPU, 512MB RAM). Most B2B teams use the High Frequency or Dedicated Cloud plans, ranging from $60 to $320/month depending on resource needs. All services are billed hourly with no minimum term.

Does Vultr have a free plan?

Vultr doesn’t offer a permanent free plan, but provides a $100 free credit for new users — enough to run a small instance for up to 30 days. No credit card is required to claim it, making it a true risk-free trial.

Is Vultr worth the price?

Yes, for most technical teams. Vultr delivers strong ROI through predictable pricing, global reach, and high-performance infrastructure. Compared to AWS or Azure, it’s 30–50% cheaper for equivalent compute. The lack of egress fees and hourly billing adds cost control, making it ideal for dev/test environments and production workloads alike.
