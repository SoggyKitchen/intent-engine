Elementor
WordPress Page Builder · from $0/mo

Elementor is the world's most popular WordPress page builder with 12M+ active installs. Its free version includes 40+ widgets and the core drag-and-drop editor. Pro plans add advanced widgets, theme building, popups, and marketing integrations.

Divi
WordPress Page Builder · from $89/yr

Divi by Elegant Themes is a premium WordPress page builder (and theme) with a visual front-end editor. All paid plans cover unlimited websites — its standout value proposition for agencies. Divi AI is available as an add-on for $18/mo.

Elementor vs Divi: Verdict at a Glance

Both are best-in-class WordPress page builders. Elementor (12M+ installs, free plan available) has a larger ecosystem and gentler learning curve. Divi ($89/yr for unlimited websites) offers better economics for agencies managing many client sites.

For most users — bloggers, small businesses, and solopreneurs — Elementor is the better starting point. Start free, upgrade to Pro ($59/yr) only when you need advanced features.

When to choose Elementor

  • You want a free plan to try before buying
  • You're building 1–3 sites and want the cheapest per-site cost
  • You need the broadest plugin compatibility (WooCommerce, ACF, etc.)
  • You're a beginner — Elementor has the largest community and most tutorials
  • You want 10,000+ free templates in the Elementor template library

When to choose Divi

  • You build 4+ WordPress client sites per year — flat $89/yr is cheaper than Elementor Pro per-site pricing at this scale
  • You want a lifetime deal ($249 one-time for unlimited sites + lifetime updates)
  • You like Divi's built-in split testing (Divi Leads) for conversion optimisation
  • You prefer Divi's integrated theme + builder combo with deep Divi-specific styling

Our Final Verdict

Bottom line

Elementor is our recommendation for most users — beginners, bloggers, SMBs, and anyone building 1–3 sites. The free plan alone makes it an obvious first choice, and the ecosystem (tutorials, plugins, templates) is unmatched.

Divi is the smarter choice for freelancers and agencies building 4+ sites per year. One $89/yr fee covers unlimited sites, and the $249 lifetime deal is one of the best pricing constructs in WordPress tooling.

Choose Elementor if:

  • You're starting out and want a free plan before committing
  • You need maximum plugin compatibility (ACFPRO, WooCommerce, JetEngine)
  • You prefer the world's largest template library and community
  • You're building 1–3 sites annually

Choose Divi if:

  • You manage 4+ client WordPress sites per year
  • You want a lifetime deal ($249, unlimited sites, lifetime updates)
  • You value built-in split testing (Divi Leads)
  • You prefer an all-in-one theme + builder combo

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Elementor better than Divi in 2026?
Elementor scores 8.4/10 vs Divi's 8.0/10 in our editorial scoring. Elementor is better for most users — free plan, larger template library, and broader plugin compatibility. Divi is better for agencies managing multiple sites ($89/yr for unlimited websites).
Is Divi cheaper than Elementor?
For a single site, Elementor Essential ($59/yr) is cheaper than Divi Annual ($89/yr). For 4+ sites, Divi is cheaper because it charges a flat fee for unlimited websites. Divi's $249 lifetime deal is the cheapest long-term option for high-volume site builders.
Can I use Elementor for free?
Yes — Elementor has a free plan (Elementor Free) available on WordPress.org with 40+ widgets and the core drag-and-drop editor. Divi does not have a free plan but offers a 30-day money-back guarantee.
What is Divi best for?
Divi is best for freelancers and agencies building many WordPress client sites. The $89/yr plan covers unlimited websites — cheaper per-site than Elementor Pro when you manage 4+ sites. The $249 lifetime deal (unlimited sites + lifetime updates) is popular with high-volume builders.
Which is easier to use — Elementor or Divi?
Elementor has a slightly gentler learning curve. Its drag-and-drop front-end editor is intuitive, and the community (12M+ installs) means thousands of tutorials exist for every use case. Divi's visual builder is also user-friendly, but the initial setup and Divi-specific concepts (rows, sections, modules) take a little longer to learn.
