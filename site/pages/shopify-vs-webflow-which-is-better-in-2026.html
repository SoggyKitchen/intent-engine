Tool Overviews

Shopify
From $29/mo · 14-day trial · 6,000+ apps

Shopify is the dominant ecommerce platform, powering over 4 million stores worldwide. It is built from the ground up for selling: Shopify Payments handles card processing in 23+ countries with no third-party fees, the admin handles inventory across multiple locations, and the app store covers everything from abandoned cart recovery to shipping label printing. Multi-channel selling lets merchants list on Amazon, eBay, Facebook, Instagram, and TikTok from a single dashboard. For anyone whose core business is selling products, Shopify is the professional standard.

Webflow
Free Starter · Ecommerce from $29/mo · Visual CMS

Webflow is a design-first visual website builder that generates clean HTML, CSS, and JavaScript without requiring developers to write it. Its visual CMS is a standout: designers can build dynamic content collections, blog posts, portfolio pieces, team members, product features, that non-technical editors update through a simple interface. Webflow's ecommerce add-on covers straightforward stores, but its design and content publishing capabilities are where it genuinely excels. Ideal for agencies, designers, and content-driven businesses that need a beautiful, maintainable site.

Pricing in Detail

Shopify has four main plans. The Starter plan ($5/mo) adds buy buttons and payment links to an existing website but does not include a standalone storefront. Basic ($29/mo) is the entry point for a full store, it includes everything most merchants need but charges a 2% transaction fee on non-Shopify payment methods. The Grow plan ($79/mo, formerly called Shopify) reduces that fee to 1% and adds better reporting. Advanced ($299/mo) drops fees to 0.5% and unlocks custom reporting. Shopify Plus starts at $2,500/mo for high-volume enterprise merchants. All plans come with a 14-day free trial, no credit card required.

Webflow separates site plans from ecommerce plans. Site plans (no commerce) run from free (Starter) to $14/mo (Basic), $23/mo (CMS), and $39/mo (Business). For a store, you need an ecommerce plan: Standard at $29/mo supports up to 500 products but charges a 2% transaction fee. Plus at $74/mo removes the transaction fee and raises the product limit to 5,000 items. Advanced at $212/mo supports unlimited products with priority support. Webflow has a free Starter plan for non-ecommerce sites, but it displays Webflow branding on the published URL.

For a like-for-like ecommerce comparison: both platforms start at $29/mo for a basic store, and both charge a 2% transaction fee on that entry plan. Above that tier, Shopify's fees drop quickly while Webflow Plus at $74/mo removes them. For a high-volume store, Shopify's fee structure becomes significantly more advantageous, especially once you factor in Shopify Payments eliminating third-party fees entirely.

Design Control & CMS

This is where Webflow is genuinely superior. Webflow's visual canvas maps directly to CSS flexbox and grid, experienced designers can achieve pixel-perfect layouts, animations, and interactions without writing a line of code. The generated HTML/CSS is clean and production-quality. For content-heavy sites, Webflow's CMS lets editors manage structured content collections (blog posts, case studies, team bios) that are beautifully rendered by the visual template the designer built.

Shopify's design system is built around Liquid, its templating language. The theme store has thousands of professional templates, and Theme Customizer covers most visual changes without code. However, going beyond the template requires Liquid knowledge. Shopify's Online Store 2.0 architecture introduced more flexible sections, but it doesn't match Webflow's design freedom. For a merchant whose competitive advantage is selling products, not design artistry, this tradeoff is acceptable. For a portfolio studio or a brand where the site is the product, Webflow wins clearly.

Ecommerce Capability

Shopify was built for ecommerce from day one, and it shows. Inventory management handles product variants, multi-location stock, low-stock alerts, and purchase orders. The checkout is heavily optimised, Shop Pay (Shopify's accelerated checkout) converts at measurably higher rates. Abandoned cart recovery, discount codes, gift cards, and subscription selling are all native features. The 6,000+ app ecosystem covers every edge case: print-on-demand, dropshipping, loyalty programs, B2B wholesale, and more.

Webflow's ecommerce capability covers standard use cases: product pages, a shopping cart, checkout via Stripe, and basic order management. It's solid for a designer building a small shop as part of a larger site. But it lacks Shopify's depth: no multi-channel selling, no multi-location inventory, fewer payment gateway options, and a much smaller app ecosystem. Webflow does not support subscription products natively. For serious ecommerce, anything beyond a small curated store, Shopify is the platform.

Final Verdict

Shopify (9.3/10) wins for anyone whose primary goal is selling products. It's purpose-built for ecommerce in a way Webflow simply isn't, built-in payments, multi-channel selling, mature inventory management, and 6,000+ apps give it an enormous advantage for serious merchants. The 14-day free trial makes it easy to test before committing.

Webflow (8.0/10) wins for designers, agencies, and businesses building content-first websites. Its visual CMS and design control are genuinely unmatched in this tier. If you need a store, Webflow's ecommerce add-on handles straightforward product catalogues, but it's a secondary capability, not the core product. Use Webflow when your site's design and content are the product; use Shopify when your store is the product.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Shopify better than Webflow?
For selling products, yes, Shopify scores 9.3/10 vs Webflow's 8.0/10. Shopify is purpose-built for ecommerce with built-in payments, 6,000+ apps, and multi-channel selling. Webflow excels at design-first websites and CMS. The right answer depends entirely on what you're building: a store or a website.
Can Webflow do ecommerce?
Yes, Webflow has ecommerce plans from $29/mo (Standard, up to 500 products with a 2% transaction fee) to $74/mo (Plus, 5,000 products, no transaction fee) and $212/mo (Advanced). It's suitable for small curated stores, but lacks Shopify's depth: no multi-channel selling, smaller app ecosystem, and no native subscription support.
How much does Shopify cost vs Webflow?
For a full ecommerce store, both start at $29/mo. Shopify Basic charges a 2% fee on non-Shopify payment methods (0% with Shopify Payments); Webflow Standard charges 2% on all transactions. Shopify's Grow plan at $79/mo reduces the fee to 1%. Webflow site-only plans start cheaper: free (Starter), $14/mo (Basic), $23/mo (CMS).
Does Shopify have a free plan?
Shopify offers a 14-day free trial with no credit card required. There's no ongoing free storefront plan. The $5/mo Starter plan lets you add buy buttons to an existing website. Webflow has a free Starter plan for non-ecommerce sites (with Webflow branding on the URL).
Which is better for SEO, Shopify or Webflow?
Both cover the fundamentals: custom meta tags, canonical URLs, sitemaps, and fast CDN hosting. Webflow gives designers more granular HTML/CSS control, which benefits content-heavy sites. Shopify's product-page SEO is strong with good schema defaults and many SEO apps available. For content-marketing-led SEO, Webflow's CMS is more flexible; for product-page SEO, Shopify's ecosystem wins.
Can I migrate from Webflow to Shopify?
Yes, Webflow ecommerce product data can be exported as a CSV and imported into Shopify. CMS content (blog posts, pages) requires manual migration or third-party tools. Most merchants report the product migration is straightforward; the main effort is rebuilding the storefront in a Shopify theme or Liquid template.
