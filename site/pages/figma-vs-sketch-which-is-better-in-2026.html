Collaboration: Figma Wins Decisively

Figma's real-time multiplayer — where multiple designers, developers, and stakeholders can view and comment simultaneously — is its killer feature. You can share a Figma link with anyone and they'll see the design in their browser, no account required for view-only.

Sketch has cloud collaboration but it's more like async file sharing than true multiplayer. Developers get Sketch Cloud for developer handoff, but it's not as seamless as Figma's Dev Mode.

Performance: Sketch Has an Edge on Mac

Sketch is a native Mac application and runs faster than Figma's browser-based renderer on complex files with many components. For files with thousands of layers, Sketch users typically report smoother scrolling and faster export times on Apple Silicon.

Figma has improved significantly and is fast for most projects. But if you have very large design systems or complex illustrations, Sketch's native performance still has an edge.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Figma better than Sketch in 2026?
Yes, for most teams. Figma is the industry standard, works on any OS, has real-time collaboration, and a generous free plan. Sketch is still preferred by some Mac-native designers for speed and native performance. Figma scores 9.2/10 vs Sketch's 7.8/10.
Is Sketch cheaper than Figma?
Yes. Sketch costs $9/editor/mo vs Figma Professional at $12/editor/mo. Figma offers a free tier; Sketch does not (30-day trial only). For cost-sensitive Mac-only teams, Sketch can save money.
Does Sketch work on Windows?
No. Sketch is Mac-only. This is its biggest limitation. Developers and stakeholders on Windows cannot use Sketch natively. Figma runs in any browser on any OS.
Can I import Sketch files into Figma?
Yes. Figma has a built-in Sketch importer. Most Sketch files migrate cleanly, though complex symbols and plugins may need re-configuration. Many teams have migrated from Sketch to Figma this way.
What are the best alternatives to Figma and Sketch?
Adobe XD (discontinued 2023), Canva (for non-designers), Framer (code-based prototyping), Penpot (open-source, self-hostable), and Affinity Designer (one-time purchase for illustration). For product design in 2026, Figma has no close second.
