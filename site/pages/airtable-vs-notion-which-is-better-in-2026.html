Airtable
Database · Free / from ~$20/seat/mo

Airtable is a spreadsheet-database hybrid with multiple view types (grid, kanban, gallery, calendar, Gantt, form). It excels at relational data — linking records across tables, rolling up data, and building custom interfaces. Used by product, ops, and marketing teams for complex data pipelines.

Notion
Docs + Database · Free / from ~$10/seat/mo

Notion combines a block-based doc editor with inline databases — every database can appear as a table, kanban, calendar, or gallery anywhere on a page. With Notion AI added to paid plans, it's become the go-to for teams that want wiki, notes, projects, and databases in one tool.

Verdict at a Glance

The core difference: Airtable is a database first, with docs bolted on as an afterthought. Notion is a document editor first, with databases embedded as blocks. This sounds subtle but changes everything about how teams actually use them day-to-day.

Notion has closed much of the database gap with linked databases, rollup properties, and formula improvements. For most B2B SaaS teams — content calendar, roadmap, company wiki, project tracker — Notion handles all of this elegantly in one workspace. Airtable still leads on relational complexity: multiple linked tables, complex formula fields, scripting extensions, and custom apps built on top of the database.

Who Should Choose Airtable?

  • Operations and data-heavy teams — Airtable's relational tables, roll-ups, and lookups handle complex data operations Notion's databases can't match.
  • Teams building internal apps — Airtable's Interface Designer lets you create no-code dashboards and apps on top of your data, shareable with external stakeholders.
  • Marketing asset management — Airtable's gallery view and attachment handling make it the go-to for content, design, or campaign asset databases.
  • Teams with existing Airtable workflows — If your team has built complex automations or formulas in Airtable, migrating to Notion would lose that structure.

Who Should Choose Notion?

  • Teams wanting a company wiki + projects in one place — Notion's block model lets you embed databases inside documents, making it far more contextual than Airtable.
  • Budget-conscious teams — Notion's paid plans start at ~$10/seat/mo vs Airtable's ~$20/seat/mo. For most use cases, Notion does more at half the price.
  • Teams adopting AI writing tools — Notion AI (included in paid plans) drafts, summarises, and rewrites content inline — there's no equivalent in Airtable.
  • Mixed teams (engineers + PMs + marketers) — Notion's flexibility means every team can have their own setup in one shared workspace without needing separate tools.

Our Final Verdict

Notion is our recommendation for most B2B SaaS teams in 2026 — better value, faster adoption, and a genuinely superior writing+wiki experience. Airtable is the right call for data-intensive operations teams who need relational database power that Notion can't match.

  • Choose Notion for company wiki, docs, project management, content calendar, roadmap, or any team that needs flexible workspaces. Start free; upgrade at ~$10/seat/mo for team sharing.
  • Choose Airtable for inventory management, content asset databases, complex relational data, or building no-code internal apps. Budget for ~$20/seat/mo.

If you need project management beyond what both offer, consider ClickUp — it blends docs and task management with more PM-specific features than either Airtable or Notion.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Airtable better than Notion?

Notion scores 8.9/10 vs Airtable's 8.7/10 for most teams. Notion wins on docs, wiki, AI, and price. Airtable wins on relational database power, formula complexity, and building internal apps on top of data.

How much does Airtable cost vs Notion?

Airtable paid plans start at ~$20/seat/mo. Notion paid plans start at ~$10/seat/mo with AI included. Notion is roughly half the price at equivalent tiers for most teams.

Can I use Airtable and Notion for free?

Both have free plans. Airtable free: unlimited bases, 1,200 records per base, 2 GB storage. Notion free: unlimited blocks for individuals, limited team sharing. Both are genuinely useful for solo users or very small teams.

Which is better for project management?

Notion is generally better for project management because you can mix documentation and task databases in one page. For teams that want more dedicated PM features (dependencies, time tracking, workload), ClickUp or Asana are stronger alternatives.

Is Notion replacing Airtable?

Notion has closed the gap significantly with linked databases and formula improvements, but Airtable still leads on relational database complexity, scripting extensions, and its Interface Designer for building custom no-code apps. They serve different primary use cases in 2026.
