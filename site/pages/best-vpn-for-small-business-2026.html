What Small Businesses Actually Need from a VPN

A consumer VPN protects one person on one device. A business VPN has to protect a whole team — including people who forget to turn it on, devices you don't control (BYOD), and cloud tools that need a static IP for whitelisting. Here's what to prioritise:

Kill Switch

Cuts internet if VPN drops — stops accidental plain-text transmission. Essential for remote employees on hotel or airport Wi-Fi.

Split Tunneling

Route only work traffic through the VPN. Stops video calls from lagging because all traffic was tunnelled through a distant server.

Dedicated IP

A fixed IP address you can whitelist in your CRM, cloud storage, and banking tools. Shared IPs often get blocked or flagged.

Centralised Billing

One invoice for all seats, not N separate subscriptions. Most business-tier plans include this; consumer plans don't.

No-Logs Audit

Third-party verification that the VPN doesn't store browsing records. NordVPN (PwC), Surfshark (Deloitte), and ProtonVPN (Securitum) are all independently verified.

SSO Integration

Single sign-on with Google Workspace, Okta, or Azure AD. Reduces onboarding friction and lets you revoke access instantly when someone leaves.

Do small businesses really need a VPN?

Yes — especially for remote teams. Without one, remote employees on coffee-shop Wi-Fi expose company credentials and client data.
