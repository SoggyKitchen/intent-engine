How ElevenLabs and HeyGen Work Together

These are not competing tools, they occupy different production layers. ElevenLabs handles the audio layer (voice generation, cloning, dubbing); HeyGen handles the video layer (avatar rendering, lip-sync, visual output). Many content teams run both: generate a voice-over in ElevenLabs at superior quality, then pass the audio to HeyGen for lip-sync against a custom avatar. HeyGen has a native ElevenLabs integration for exactly this workflow.

When to run them together: your avatar video needs a brand-consistent voice that HeyGen's built-in options can't match; or you're producing multilingual content at scale and want ElevenLabs' language coverage on the voice track while HeyGen handles visual localisation. Studios running a consistent video series (same presenter, multiple episodes) often batch all audio through ElevenLabs, then upload to HeyGen for identical avatar videos with consistent voice across languages.

Pricing in Detail

ElevenLabs prices by character count (one character ≈ one letter or space). Free gives 10,000 characters/month, roughly 7–8 minutes of audio, with no credit card. Starter ($5/mo, 30,000 chars) covers about 20 minutes of audio monthly: enough for a weekly short podcast or daily short-form content. Creator ($22/mo, 100,000 chars) handles high-volume narration and audiobook production. Pro ($99/mo, 500,000 chars) and Scale ($330/mo, 2,000,000 chars) serve enterprise TTS pipelines and API-heavy applications.

HeyGen prices by videos per month and maximum video length. Free gives 1 minute of video total per month, watermarked, good for a first look, not production. Creator ($29/mo) allows 15 videos per month up to 3 minutes each. Team ($89/mo, 3 seats) doubles the allowance to 30 videos/month, extends the length cap to 5 minutes per video, and adds collaboration features.

The pricing gap is significant: ElevenLabs Starter at $5/month versus HeyGen Creator at $29/month. But these produce fundamentally different outputs, audio versus video. The comparison only matters if you're deciding which layer to invest in first, or whether to run both.
