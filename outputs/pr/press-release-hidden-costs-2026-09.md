=== PRESS RELEASE HEADLINE (max 80 chars) ===
HubSpot's $1,500 Onboarding Fee Is Required: New SaaSpare Pricing Data

=== SUMMARY (max 45 words) ===
SaaSpare has published verified pricing research covering 15 major B2B SaaS vendors. It documents required onboarding fees, seat minimums and annual-only billing terms that push real costs well above advertised per-seat prices. Every figure was read from the vendors' own pricing pages and dated.

=== PRESS RELEASE BODY ===
BRISBANE, Australia — SaaSpare, an independent B2B software comparison publisher, has released pricing research showing that the advertised per-seat price of major SaaS products routinely understates what a buyer actually pays in year one.

The research covers 15 vendors including HubSpot, Salesforce, monday.com, Ahrefs, Semrush, Shopify and 1Password. Each figure was read directly from the vendor's own public pricing page on 6 September 2026, and every entry on the published page links back to its source.

Among the findings:

HubSpot's Sales Hub Professional plan advertises $100 per seat per month. Its pricing page also describes Professional Onboarding at $1,500 as a required, one-time fee. For a single seat, that makes year one $2,700 rather than $1,200. The equivalent Enterprise onboarding fee is $3,500.

monday.com states that plans start from three users. A single user cannot buy a paid plan at all, so the entry price is three times the advertised per-seat rate. Captured from an Australian connection, that rate was $14 AUD per seat, making the real floor $42 AUD per month. monday.com's pricing page displays local currency, so the figure a buyer sees varies by country; the three-seat minimum does not.

Every Salesforce Sales Cloud plan above Starter Suite is marked "Billed annually." Pro Suite reads as $100 per seat per month and is invoiced as $1,200 per seat up front. Core, Advanced and Max reach $6,600 per seat per year.

The gap between monthly and annual billing varies sharply, even inside a single vendor. HubSpot's Sales Hub Starter is $7 per seat annually against $20 monthly — paying monthly costs 186 percent more. On Professional the same gap is 11 percent.

Ahrefs includes one user on each plan and charges $40 to $80 per month for each additional seat, so a three-person team on its Advanced plan pays substantially more than the $449 headline.

"None of this is concealed," said Kaylan von Papen, founder of SaaSpare. "Every one of these charges is published by the vendor. The problem is that a $1,500 onboarding fee appears once, in small type, under a very large per-seat number. Buyers budget from the number in the heading. We wanted a single page where the rest of it is visible before you sign."

SaaSpare states plainly what the research is not. The publisher does not run usability, performance or deliverability testing, and does not report any such results. The work is documentary: reading published pricing pages, recording what they say, and dating it. Where a vendor charges no additional fee, the page records that finding explicitly rather than omitting the vendor — monday.com and Salesforce are both listed as charging no onboarding fee.

Alongside the fee data, SaaSpare publishes a Cost Transparency Score rating each vendor out of five on eight criteria: whether it publishes a real price, whether it requires a setup fee, whether it will sell a single seat, whether it permits monthly billing, whether it offers a free tier, whether its trial requires a credit card, whether monthly billing carries a penalty, and whether extra seats cost the advertised rate. Asana, Datadog, Linear and Stripe score full marks. Ahrefs scores lowest at 9 out of 16.

The scores are editorial ratings published by SaaSpare, computed from the pricing data. They are not user reviews, and SaaSpare does not host user reviews or publish user ratings.

SaaSpare accepts affiliate commissions from some vendors it covers and discloses this on every monetised page. It states that no vendor has paid to influence a score or verdict, and that scores are recomputed from the underlying data rather than set by hand.

The full research is available at https://saaspare.org/pages/saas-hidden-costs-2026 and the transparency scores at https://saaspare.org/pages/saas-pricing-transparency-scores-2026.
