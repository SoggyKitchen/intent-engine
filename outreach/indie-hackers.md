## Milestone Post — Good for gaining traction early

**Title:** I just hit 1,000 pages on my SaaS comparison site — here's what I learned building it

**Body:**
After about 12 months of building, SaaSpare just crossed 1,000 comparison and pricing pages. A few things I'd share with anyone building a similar project:

**What worked:**
- Programmatic generation is essential. I wrote a Python pipeline that generates pages from structured YAML data. Without it, 1,000 pages isn't humanly possible.
- Niche first. The most-trafficked pages are ultra-specific: "HubSpot vs Pipedrive for a 10-seat sales team" rather than "best CRM." Long-tail works.
- Actual pricing. Most comparison sites avoid showing numbers because they're too lazy to maintain them. We do it anyway. It's our main differentiator.
- Free tools pull traffic. The Shortlist Builder and ROI Calculator both get organic visits from Google even with low domain authority.

**What's harder than expected:**
- Affiliate program quality is inconsistent. Some vendors take 90+ days to approve. Others have broken tracking.
- Trust takes a long time. Early traffic is mostly bots and scrapers. Real users come slowly.
- Content freshness is ongoing work. SaaS pricing changes constantly. I now run a monthly price-check pass.

Happy to answer questions. Still in early growth phase but the data side is genuinely interesting.

Link: saaspare.org

## Data Post — Linkbait style

**Title:** I found that SaaS prices rose 34% across all categories in 3 years — here's the breakdown

**Body:**
As part of building SaaSpare, I tracked pricing across 1,000+ tools going back to public records from 2023. The aggregate numbers are striking.

**Biggest inflations:**
- Zendesk: +135% per agent
- HubSpot Pro: +98% (from $450 to $890/mo for 5-seat min)
- Monday: +63% per seat

**What drives this:**
1. Post-IPO / post-growth-round margin pressure
2. Usage-based pricing being converted to seat-based (pricing power move)
3. Free-tier restrictions pushing more users into paid

The full research report with methodology is at saaspare.org/research/saas-pricing-2026 — licensed CC BY so anyone can use the data.
